Choosing your business structure
The most important first decision — and luckily, the simplest for most micro-entrepreneurs.
Beginner15 min readOne-time decision
🏗️
What is a business structure?
How your business is legally organised
Your business structure determines how you pay taxes, your personal liability if something goes wrong, and how easy it is to get bank accounts or loans. For most home-based micro-entrepreneurs starting out, there are three realistic options:

1. Sole Proprietorship — You and the business are legally the same person. The simplest structure, no formal registration required (though you should register for GST, Shops & Establishment, or MSME). Best for: tailors, tutors, beauticians, repair technicians, food makers.

2. One Person Company (OPC) — A single-person company with limited liability. More complex to set up, requires a company registration. Best when you expect high turnover (above ₹40–50 lakh) or want formal investor interest.

3. Partnership Firm — Two or more people running a business together. Requires a Partnership Deed. Best when starting a business with a family member or trusted friend.
✅ Recommendation for 95% of EntreSkill users: Start as a Sole Proprietorship. It requires zero formal registration to begin, costs nothing, and you can always upgrade to an OPC or Private Limited Company later when you outgrow it.

Business registration — step by step
Which registrations do you actually need, and in what order?
Beginner20 min readOne-time
📋
The recommended registration order
Do these in sequence — each one builds on the previous
1
Udyam / MSME Registration (Free — do this first)
Visit udyamregistration.gov.in, enter your Aadhaar and PAN. Takes 10 minutes. Gives you an official MSME certificate which banks accept as business proof.
2
Shops & Establishment Registration (₹200–₹1,000)
Required if you operate from a premises (even home-based in many states). Apply at your local municipal corporation or labour department. Needed for bank accounts.
3
Business Bank Account
Now that you have your MSME certificate and Shops & Establishment license, open a current account at any bank. Keep business money separate from personal money — this is non-negotiable.
4
GST Registration (if needed)
Mandatory only if annual turnover exceeds ₹20 lakh (₹10 lakh for special category states). Optional but recommended if your clients are businesses who need GST invoices.
5
Trade License (for specific businesses)
Food businesses need FSSAI. Pharmacies, salons, and some repair shops may need a trade or health license from local authorities. Check your city's requirements.
📄
Documents you'll need
Collect these before starting any registration
🪪Aadhaar card (original)
💳PAN card
🏠Address proof (electricity bill / rent agreement)
📸2 passport-size photographs
🏦Bank account details (for MSME)
📱Mobile number linked to Aadhaar

GST basics for micro-entrepreneurs
Everything you need to know about GST — without the jargon.
Intermediate25 min readMonthly compliance
🧾
Do you need GST registration?
The simple answer
Mandatory: Yes, if your annual business turnover exceeds ₹20 lakh (₹40 lakh for goods sellers; ₹10 lakh for special category states like Manipur, Uttarakhand, etc.)

Voluntary: You can register even below the threshold. Advantages: issue GST invoices to business clients, claim input tax credit on your purchases, appear more professional.
💡 Rule of thumb: If more than 20% of your clients are businesses (not individuals), get GST registration even if your turnover is below ₹20 lakh. It makes you eligible for their vendor lists and input tax credit.
⚠️ Once registered, you must file GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B every month, even if you had zero sales. Missing filings attracts penalties of ₹50/day (minimum ₹200 per return).

MSME / Udyam Registration
The single most valuable free registration for any micro-entrepreneur in India.
BeginnerFree10 min to complete
🏭
What is MSME / Udyam registration?
Udyam Registration (formerly MSME registration) is a government certificate that officially classifies your business as a Micro, Small, or Medium Enterprise. It is completely free, done entirely online at udyamregistration.gov.in, and takes about 10 minutes with just your Aadhaar and PAN.

Benefits of MSME registration:
  • Priority lending from banks at lower interest rates (PM Mudra Yojana, Stand-Up India)
  • Collateral-free loans up to ₹10 lakh (CGTMSE scheme)
  • Government tenders — 25% reserved for MSMEs
  • Protection against delayed payments from buyers (MSMED Act)
  • Tax benefits and subsidies under various state and central schemes
  • Accepted as business proof for opening bank accounts
✅ Do this today. Udyam registration is free, fast, and the benefits are enormous. There is absolutely no reason not to register. Go to udyamregistration.gov.in right now.

Business banking essentials
Why a separate business account is non-negotiable — and how to open one.
Beginner15 min read
🏦
Why you must separate business and personal finances
Mixing personal and business money is the #1 financial mistake new entrepreneurs make. Here's why separation matters:
  • Tax filing becomes impossible — you can't tell which expenses are business vs personal
  • Profit is invisible — you don't know if your business is actually making money
  • Clients don't trust you — paying to a personal account looks unprofessional
  • Loans are harder — banks need 6 months of business bank statements for loans
  • GST compliance fails — all GST transactions must flow through a business account
⚠️ Open your business bank account in the first month. Do not wait until you're "big enough." You're big enough right now.
📋
Documents needed to open a sole proprietorship current account
🪪PAN card
🪪Aadhaar card
🏠Business address proof
🏭MSME / Udyam certificate
📋Shops & Establishment license
💰Initial deposit (varies by bank)
Tip: SBI and Bank of Baroda have zero-balance business accounts for MSME-registered businesses. HDFC and ICICI offer better digital banking but require higher minimum balances.

Contracts, invoices & client agreements
Protect yourself and look professional — even as a solo operator.
Intermediate20 min read
📄
Every invoice must include these elements
1
Your business name, address, and contact
2
Client's name and address
3
Invoice number (sequential: INV-001, INV-002…)
4
Date of invoice and due date
5
Itemised list of services/products with amounts
6
GST amount separately shown (if GST-registered)
7
Payment terms (e.g. "Due within 7 days of delivery")
8
Bank account / UPI details for payment
💡 Use free tools: Zoho Invoice (free), Wave (free), or even Google Docs templates. Professional invoices build trust and speed up payment.
🤝
Simple client agreement — what to include
For any order above ₹2,000, send a brief written agreement (even on WhatsApp) confirming:
  • Scope of work — exactly what you will deliver
  • Timeline — when you will deliver
  • Price — total cost and payment schedule
  • Advance payment — always take 30–50% upfront for custom work
  • Revision policy — how many changes are included
  • Cancellation terms — what happens if the client cancels
A WhatsApp message saying "Confirming our order: 1 custom blouse in blue fabric, delivery by May 20, total ₹1,200, advance ₹600 received" is a legally binding agreement in India.

How sole proprietorship income is taxed
As a sole proprietor, your business income is treated as your personal income. You file ITR-3 (or ITR-4 under presumptive taxation). Tax slabs for FY 2025–26:

Income slabTax rate (New regime)
Up to ₹3,00,000Nil
₹3,00,001 – ₹7,00,0005%
₹7,00,001 – ₹10,00,00010%
₹10,00,001 – ₹12,00,00015%
₹12,00,001 – ₹15,00,00020%
Above ₹15,00,00030%
💡 Presumptive Taxation (Section 44ADA): If your professional income is below ₹50 lakh, you can declare 50% of your gross receipts as profit and pay tax only on that — no need to maintain detailed books. Huge simplification for freelancers and service providers.

Hiring & basic labour law
What you need to know before hiring your first helper or assistant.
IntermediateWhen scaling
👷
Employee vs contractor — which to hire?
Contract worker / freelancer: Pay per task or day. No PF, ESI, or long-term obligations. Best for occasional help (e.g. a helper during wedding season).

Full-time employee: Fixed monthly salary. You're legally required to pay PF (12% of basic salary) and ESI (if salary below ₹21,000/month) once you have 10+ employees.

For most home micro-businesses: Start with a contract arrangement. Pay a daily or per-piece rate. Keep a simple written agreement specifying the work, rate, and that they're an independent contractor.
💡 Recommendation: Start by hiring a contract helper 2–3 days/week. Once your order volume consistently exceeds your capacity for 2+ months, consider a full-time hire.
